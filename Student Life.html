Traditions & Annual Events

  • The Fog Run — At the first heavy fog of autumn (usually early October), seniors run a barefoot lap around the quad at dawn. No one announces it in advance; a bell in Founders Tower just starts ringing and word spreads dorm to dorm.
  • Rooting Day — Each graduating class plants a tree in the arboretum before commencement. Alumni returning for reunions make a point of visiting "their" tree.
  • Lantern Crossing — At the winter solstice, students carry paper lanterns from the library across the footbridge to the old millpond, a tradition that started as a blackout-era safety measure and stuck around for the atmosphere.

  • The Marginalia Hunt — Once a semester, staff hide handwritten notes and doodles inside random library books; finding one gets you a byline in the student lit magazine of the same name.

Housing

Dorms are named after the town's vanished industries — Millhouse, Foundry Hall, Tannery Row — each with its own minor identity: Millhouse is known for an ongoing, decade-spanning game of hallway chess played by mail-slot notes; Foundry Hall runs the loudest common-room movie nights; Tannery Row has a reputation (fair or not) for being where the campus's serious birdwatchers live, on account of its proximity to Alder Hollow

Food & Hangouts

The dining hall, The Hearth, does a standing Thursday-night "bring a family recipe" swap where students submit dishes to be added to the rotation. Off campus, The Cartographer's Table — a diner named half-jokingly after the map-studies program — is the unofficial 2 a.m. study spot.

Clubs & Organizations

  • Marginalia (lit magazine, publishing only what's "found" in library books)
  • The Rope Line — canopy-climbing and tree-work club, overlapping heavily with the Canopy Ecology program
  • Wrenfield Board Game Society — runs open playtesting nights for the Comparative Board Game Studies capstone projects
  • The Night Choir — an a cappella group that only performs outdoors, after dark
  • County Stories Project — folklore students and volunteers who record oral histories from Wrenfield's older residents
